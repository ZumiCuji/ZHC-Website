Project Lazarus Quantitative Trading Intelligence

Project Lazarus is a multi-agent algorithmic trading system, built in Python, that scans live equity and index markets for intraday 0DTE options setups, executes them through a brokerage paper-trading API, manages every position with automated risk logic, and reports the whole thing in real time through Discord. It began as a signal-research experiment and grew into a full execution, risk-management, and analytics platform. This page explains what it does — and how it was built.

Read the numbers honestly

Every performance figure on this page comes from backtesting and paper (simulated) trading — not a track record of real, executed capital. Backtested results are hypothetical: they assume clean fills and can flatter a strategy in hindsight. The one-week run that carried a $500 paper balance to $1,613.35 is an extremely small sample on a deliberately high-risk 0DTE options strategy — a result like that is driven far more by variance and a handful of outlier trades than by a proven edge, and runs like it routinely reverse.

Paper trading also leaves out the real-world frictions that decide whether a strategy actually works: live slippage, the pattern-day-trader rule, execution on a 15-minute-delayed data feed, and the discipline cost of trading real money. None of this is investment advice or an offer of any kind. Past and simulated performance does not predict future results, and 0DTE options can lose their entire value within a single trading day.

How It Works

Lazarus processes synchronized 5-minute and 15-minute market feeds across QQQ, SPY, SPX, NVDA, META, TSLA, and AAPL through custom technical-analysis pipelines, reversal-detection logic, and automated risk management.

Multi-Timeframe Engine

Analyzes 5m and 15m OHLCV structure together — EMA crossovers, VWAP positioning, RSI momentum, and abnormal volume expansion — so signals have to agree across timeframes before they fire.

Signal Intelligence

Detects breakout continuation, VWAP rejections, failed opening-range breaks, RSI divergences, and EMA reclaim/loss structures, splitting them into momentum and reversal setups.

Execution Layer

Selects ATM and far-OTM 0DTE contracts from the broker's own option chain, sizes against a shared daily budget, and places real (paper) orders, confirming the actual fill before it tracks the trade.

Automated Risk Management

Manages each position live with volatility-tiered stops and targets, scaled profit-taking, breakeven runner stops, and a hard end-of-day close — then verifies every exit actually closed on the broker.

How It Was Built

Lazarus came together in distinct phases — from a signal-research idea to an autonomous, remotely-operated system grounded entirely in live brokerage data.

01

Signal Research & Strategy Design

It started with a thesis: that intraday momentum and exhaustion leave a readable footprint across timeframes. I defined the rules — multi-timeframe EMA structure, VWAP positioning, RSI, and volume expansion — for two opposite ideas: breakout continuation and far-OTM reversal.

02

Backtesting & Replay Framework

Before risking anything, I built a replay engine to run the rules over 45 days of historical data, producing trade-by-trade P&L and analytics. This is where the simulated figures come from — useful for pressure-testing logic, but hypothetical by nature.

03

Real-Time Bot Architecture

The strategy split into two independent Discord bots — a momentum "Main" engine and an asymmetric "Lotto" engine — each scanning live feeds, calling out setups, and dispatching structured alerts the moment a signal fires.

04

Live Execution via Alpaca

I wired the bots into Alpaca's paper-trading API so signals became actual orders — contract selection, position sizing against a shared daily budget, fills, and account-level P&L — turning a scanner into a system that actually trades.

05

Grounding Everything in Real Data

The hardest and most important phase: I replaced every internally-estimated price with live brokerage data. Confirmed fill prices on entry, real unrealized P&L on exit, position-truth reads on every command, and finally contract selection from the broker's own option chain — so the strike chosen and the price reasoned about come from the same feed that fills and marks the trade.

06

Risk & Trade Management

Added volatility-tiered stops and targets, scaled profit-taking — trim the bulk at the first target and let one or two "runner" contracts ride with the stop moved to breakeven — and focused new entries into the highest-movement part of the session, backed by data.

07

Honest Analytics

I built reporting that measures the strategy rather than flatters it: per-leg accounting that separates trimmed legs from runners, and a time-of-day study that finds when the underlying actually moves. The point is to surface the truth about the edge, including when there isn't one.

08

Autonomous Operations

Finally, I made it run itself: scheduled with systemd, fully operable from a phone over a private mesh network and owner-locked Discord admin commands, with self-deploy and a watchdog that auto-rolls-back a bad update if the bot stops sending heartbeats.
